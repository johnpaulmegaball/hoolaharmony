The Importance of Relationships

Week ofNov 23

Relationships are very important to our mental health because we naturally need connection, support, and a sense of belonging. The people we surround ourselves with can influence how we think, feel, behave, and view ourselves.

Why are they important?

Emotional support

Feeling understood when things get hard.

Sense of belonging

Feeling accepted, valued, and less alone.

Stress relief

Talking it out makes problems feel lighter.

Personal growth

Building empathy, patience, and communication.

Self-esteem

A reminder of our own worth.

How do relationships affect mental health?

Positive relationships…

  • Increase feelings of happiness and security
  • Provide encouragement and reassurance
  • Help reduce feelings of loneliness
  • Create a safe space to express emotions

Negative relationships…

  • Can contribute to stress and anxiety
  • May lower self-esteem and confidence
  • Can create feelings of insecurity or loneliness
  • May make it difficult to express yourself freely

Important reminders

You do not need hundreds of friends to feel connected — a few relationships can make a difference. Asking for support is a sign of self-awareness, not weakness. It's okay to outgrow certain relationships. Healthy relationships should let you feel respected and accepted. And you can care about someone while still establishing boundaries.

The basics

What is mental health?

Mental health is our emotional, psychological, and social well-being. It affects how we think, feel, and act, how we handle stress, and how we connect with others. Everyone has mental health, just like everyone has physical health.

Mental health is a range, and it can change

Thriving

Normal mood swings, sleeping well, feeling connected and able to focus.

Surviving

Nervous or irritable, trouble sleeping, procrastinating, feeling a bit off.

Struggling

Ongoing sadness or worry, pulling away from friends, falling behind.

In crisis

Can't get through the day, feeling hopeless. Reach out for help now.

Mental health vs. mental illness

Mental health is something everyone has. A mental illness is a diagnosable condition, like anxiety, depression, or ADHD, that affects thinking, mood, or behavior. Mental illnesses are common and treatable.

Signs to check in on

  • Big changes in sleep or appetite
  • Pulling away from friends or activities
  • Feeling sad, hopeless, or worried most days
  • Trouble concentrating in class
  • Talking about self-harm or not wanting to be here

Healthy coping strategies

Tools for hard days

Coping skills help you get through stress without making things worse later. Try a few and keep the ones that work for you.

5-4-3-2-1 grounding

Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, and 1 you taste. It brings you back to the present.

Move your body

A walk, a swim, stretching, or dancing in your room. Movement lowers stress hormones.

Connect with someone

Text a friend, talk story with family, or sit with someone you trust.

Get it out on paper

Journal, draw, or scrapbook. Putting feelings somewhere outside your head makes them easier to sort through.

Play music

Listen or play. Check out our Spotify playlist.

Rest and routine

8 to 10 hours of sleep, regular meals, and breaks from your phone help your mood more than you'd think.

Ready

Try box breathing

Breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 4, breathe out for 4, hold for 4. Repeat a few times. It slows your heart rate and calms your nervous system.

Healthy coping

  • Talking to someone you trust
  • Exercise and time outside
  • Creative hobbies
  • Breathing and grounding
  • Asking a counselor for help

Coping that can backfire

  • Bottling everything up
  • Scrolling for hours to avoid feelings
  • Skipping sleep or meals
  • Isolating from everyone
  • Using substances to numb out

Need to talk to someone now?

Free, confidential, and available 24/7. You can also talk to your school counselor or a trusted adult.
